Point-and-click adventure (2013–2020), developed and published by Cardboard Computer (TV Edition by Annapurna Interactive). Platinum trophy earned.

![](/posts/2026-1104-kentucky-route-zero.webp)

## Plot Summary (Spoilers)

1. Conway, an antique-shop delivery driver, takes one last job: deliver an antique to 5 Dogwood Drive. The address doesn't exist on any map — the only way there is a secret highway running through the caves beneath Kentucky: Route Zero.
2. At the Equus Oils gas station, an old man points him to the Márquez farm. Weaver Márquez gives him directions through a TV screen, then vanishes. The route leads into the abandoned Elkhorn Mine, where Conway meets Weaver's cousin Shannon, a TV repairwoman searching the flooded tunnels. A collapse injures Conway's leg.
3. Above ground, the Bureau of Reclaimed Spaces demands paperwork before giving directions. Searching for Doctor Truman to treat the leg, the group passes through the Museum of Dwellings — built where his house stood — and a strange forest, joined by Ezra, a boy whose brother is a giant eagle. Truman treats the leg with the anesthetic Neurypnol TM.
4. Conway wakes to find his injured leg replaced by a glowing skeletal limb. He's also been tricked into drinking a priceless whiskey at an underground distillery staffed by glowing skeletons, and is now in debt to work for them.
5. The musicians Johnny and Junebug join the crew for the ferry journey down the underground Echo River — tiki bars, phone booths, a flooded train-tunnel telephone exchange. Conway's transformation completes: he turns fully into a skeleton and departs with the others.
6. Shannon unloads Conway's truck at the Silo of Late Reflections — a well at the center of an effigy mound in a flood-wrecked town where 5 Dogwood Drive stands pristine. The town buries its two dead horses and debates rebuilding. The delivery is made; everyone gathers inside the house.

## The Ending, Explained (Spoilers)

1. Conway's transformation = debt consuming him. The glowing skeletons are workers owned by the distillery; after one unpayable drink, his body becomes theirs limb by limb until he joins their eternal shift.
2. Shannon is the real protagonist. She faces her family's loss (the miners' helmets in Act I), inherits Conway's truck, and completes his delivery — proving a debt can be ended by the living instead of inherited forever.
3. 5 Dogwood Drive was real all along. Four acts of underworld for one ordinary house — the destination was less a place than a discharge of obligations.
4. The flood hit while they were underground. The town debates rebuilding or leaving; the game never answers — only that the choice is made together.
5. The final scene — the found family moving Conway's antiques into the house — is the thesis: what survives us is finished by people who care, not by the company that owns us. "I'm Going That Way" says it in one line.

## My Thoughts (Spoilers)

1. The story and dialogue were too abstract for me. Characters answer questions with riddles, nothing gets explained, and major events just happen as imagery.
2. The visuals were great — flat theatrical staging, bold typography, gorgeous scenes. Worth seeing even if the story doesn't land.
3. Honestly, the easy platinum is the only reason I finished — I'm a completionist. The journey itself was painful: boring and slow, with the trophies dragging me through a story I wasn't clicking with.
4. No voice acting, text only. I might have gotten into the story more if there were voices — reading walls of abstract dialogue made it easier to drift off.
