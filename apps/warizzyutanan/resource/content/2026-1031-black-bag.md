Spy thriller (2025). Written by David Koepp.

## Plot Summary (Spoilers)

- British intelligence officer George Woodhouse must find who leaked the top-secret malware "Severus" — one of five suspects is his own wife, Kathryn.
- At a drugged dinner party, secrets spill: Freddie is cheating on Clarissa, who stabs his hand with a steak knife. Their superior Meacham dies that night.
- George finds a planted movie stub and a Zurich bank account with £7M in Kathryn's name — she's being framed.
- Redirecting a spy satellite to watch Kathryn lets a Russian general slip away with a copy of Severus, planning a nuclear meltdown in Russia to end the war in Ukraine.
- Kathryn leaks the general's location to the CIA; a drone strike kills him in Poland.
- Two plots are revealed: James and boss Stieglitz leaked Severus for the meltdown; Zoe and Freddie worked to stop it, using Kathryn.
- At a second dinner, James grabs the table gun, confesses, and fires at George — blanks. Kathryn shoots him dead with a real gun.
- The couple warn everyone: never exploit their marriage again. Stieglitz is told to retire. The £7M is left untouched in Kathryn's account — George's loyalty is to his wife before his country.

<summary><strong>Ending explained (full spoilers)</strong></summary>

<ul>
<li><strong>There were two separate plans running at once.</strong> Plot 1: James (backed by boss Stieglitz) leaked Severus so a Russian general could melt down a Russian nuclear reactor — thousands of deaths, but it would destabilize the government and end the war in Ukraine. Plot 2: Zoe learned of it from James and, on moral grounds, recruited Freddie to quietly stop it.</li>
<li><strong>Why Kathryn was framed.</strong> James planted the movie stub in George's trash, lured her to Zurich, and opened the £7M account in her name — making it look like she sold Severus, and goading George into spying on her.</li>
<li><strong>The satellite redirect was the real trick.</strong> During the few minutes George pointed the spy satellite at Kathryn, the Russian general escaped his safehouse with the Severus copy. Framing Kathryn was the distraction that enabled the theft.</li>
<li><strong>How Kathryn stopped it.</strong> She suspected Stieglitz and knew reporting it up the chain would get buried, so she leaked the general's location to a CIA contact herself — a crime, but the drone strike ended the meltdown plan.</li>
<li><strong>The final dinner.</strong> George reveals both plots on a hidden recording. Cornered, James grabs the table gun, confesses (including killing Meacham), and fires at George — it's loaded with blanks. Kathryn pulls a real gun and kills him, proving the film's thesis: they'd literally kill for each other.</li>
<li><strong>The loose ends are the point.</strong> James's body goes in a lake, staged as a work casualty. Stieglitz is told to retire "feet first." And the untouched £7M stays in Kathryn's name — George never reports it. His loyalty is to his wife before his country. It's a marriage story in a spy-thriller suit.</li>
</ul>

## My Thoughts (Spoilers)

- Liked it. Short and tight at 1h30m.
- The Blanchett–Fassbender pairing was so cool, and the other cast were great too.
- Soderbergh's stylish directing, as in Ocean's. The cinematography was great.
- The plot was hard to follow, but not bad — I blamed myself for not fully understanding it. Reading the explanation afterwards made it land better.
