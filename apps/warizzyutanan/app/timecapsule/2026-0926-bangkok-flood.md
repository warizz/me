Rain began at 4pm on Thursday and barely stopped for two days. By Saturday morning, eastern Bangkok was heavily flooded: every canal on that side of the city was full, and 15 main roads were deep under water. All 50 Bangkok districts were declared disaster zones to speed up the flood response. More water was still flowing in from Nonthaburi and Pathum Thani, and a low-pressure system kept rain circulating over Greater Bangkok. Western Bangkok, on the other side of the Chao Phraya River, stayed dry.

### Thu 24 Sep, 4:00pm — the rain begins

Persistent rain starts over Bangkok and keeps going. A low-pressure area settles in — its centre tilted towards the eastern side of the capital — feeding moisture across Greater Bangkok.

*Sources: [Bangkok Post, 26 Sep 2026](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/3326513/eastern-bangkok-under-water) · [The Nation, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40071486)*

### Fri 25 Sep — the warnings stack up

- Overnight rainfall reaches **133mm**, plus another 30mm through the day, across Khlong Sam Wa, Sai Mai, Min Buri and eastern areas to Lat Krabang
- **TMD forecast**: rain over 80% of central and eastern Thailand, including Bangkok; heavy to very heavy rain nationwide through Sep 27, flash-flood risks and rough seas — small boats in the upper Gulf told to stay ashore
- **Pathum Thani on red alert**: Rangsit canal at critical level with 28 million m³ of accumulated water upstream
- **Khao Yai National Park closed** for two days (Sep 26–27) after flash floods raised landslide and rockfall risk
- **Gov. Chadchart** warns residents along eastern canals — Min Buri, Lat Krabang, Sai Mai especially — to move belongings to higher ground
- BMA runs pumping and drainage at full capacity: Phra Nakhon-side pumps moving **1,200 m³/s**, pushing canal water through Saen Saep, Phra Ong Chao and Prawet canals towards the Phra Khanong sluice gate and the Chao Phraya
- Government orders **24-hour health monitoring** in flood-risk areas

### Sat 26 Sep — eastern Bangkok under water

Nearly 300mm accumulated in Min Buri, Klong Sam Wa and Saphan Sung by morning. Governor Chadchart Sittipunt's morning briefing:

- **All 50 districts declared disaster zones** to accelerate the flood response
- All eastern canals full — pumping can't keep up; people near canals at risk
- Floodwater flowing into the capital from Nonthaburi and Pathum Thani
- 15 main roads heavily inundated; waist-deep water at Bang Kapi intersection near Saen Saep canal
- **Cell broadcast alert at 9:22am** — DDPM warning: canal water levels critical and rising, especially canal-side and low-lying areas; move belongings and valuables up, avoid travel
- **Phetchaburi Road (Makkasan–Asok)** — Saen Saep canal water overflowed onto the roadway on top of the rain; floodwater submerged car wheels, leaving cars and motorcycles stalled mid-road for officials to push clear; motorcyclists rode the sidewalks to get through
- **CAAT warned** travellers to Don Mueang and Suvarnabhumi to allow extra time — flooded roads and poor weather disrupting travel and delaying some flights
- Shoppers stock up as flooding empties convenience-store shelves; Commerce Ministry orders supply and price checks in flood-hit areas
- Bedridden people in flooded areas to be moved to hospitals; shelters open at BMA schools
- Sandbags available at district offices; if rain stops, water should recede within ~6 hours

### Sun 27 Sep — risk lingers

The low-pressure system is expected to shift gradually westwards and away, with conditions likely easing after Sep 27. Until then the flood-risk warning for eastern Bangkok stands — residents near canals should keep valuables and vehicles on higher ground. BMA coordination continues with Samut Prakan and Chachoengsao, both also dealing with severe flooding; water cannot simply be pushed from Khlong Prawet east into Chachoengsao because irrigation canals and farmland stand in the way (Royal Irrigation Department controls that flow).
